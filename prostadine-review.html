Marketing claims vs. what we could verify

  • "Supports normal prostate function." Structure/function wording is allowed with the FDA disclaimer, but it must still be truthful and substantiated under FTC rules. We found no substantiation specific to this formula.
  • "Thousands of men choose Prostadine." No source found [VERIFY].
  • Customer reviews. Testimonials are not evidence of effectiveness. The FTC's 2024 rule prohibits fake or misleadingly presented reviews.
  • "Official" site. Lookalike sites exist for many supplements. Confirm the seller's identity, address and refund terms before ordering.

Who might consider it, and who should skip it

It may suit an adult man without thyroid disease who has had urinary symptoms evaluated and understands this is a supplement, not treatment. Skip it, or ask a clinician first, if you have a thyroid condition or take thyroid medicine, take blood thinners, have undiagnosed urinary symptoms, or would use it instead of seeing a doctor.

What would change our assessment

Published human data on the finished product, full dose disclosure, and independent contaminant and iodine testing. Related: how it's described to work, side effects, and how to evaluate any prostate supplement.
