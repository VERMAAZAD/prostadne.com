Prostadine ingredients: what the evidence shows and what to know about safety

By [AUTHOR NAME, credentials] · Medically reviewed by [REVIEWER NAME, credentials], [date] · Updated [date]

Short answer: Prostadine lists nine ingredients: nori, wakame, kelp, bladderwrack, saw palmetto, pomegranate, iodine, shilajit and neem. Only saw palmetto has large human trials for urinary symptoms, and the best of them found no benefit over placebo. Four ingredients are seaweeds or iodine, which raises a question about total iodine intake. The iodine question

The NIH Office of Dietary Supplements gives the adult RDA for iodine as 150 mcg and the upper limit as 1,100 mcg per day. Seaweeds range from tens of micrograms per gram in nori to over 1,000 in kombu kelp, and kelp supplements vary widely. Chronic excess can cause hypothyroidism, hyperthyroidism or thyroiditis in susceptible people. A formula with several seaweeds plus added iodine stacks sources of one nutrient, so read the iodine line on the label.

Bottom line

The homepage describes these ingredients as "supporting" prostate and urinary wellness. The research we can cite does not show that for most of them, and for saw palmetto it largely points the other way. "Traditionally used" is not evidence of effect.
