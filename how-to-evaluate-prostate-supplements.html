How to evaluate a prostate supplement: an 8-step checklist

By [AUTHOR NAME, credentials] · Medically reviewed by [REVIEWER NAME, credentials], [date] · Updated [date]

Short answer: Before buying, check that the claims match what the FDA allows, the ingredients have human evidence at the product's dose, the product has been independently tested, and your symptoms have been properly evaluated. Most supplements fail at least one.

What supplements can and can't claim

In the U.S., supplements may make general structure/function statements such as "supports prostate health," with an FDA disclaimer. They may not claim to diagnose, treat, cure or prevent a disease like BPH or prostate cancer, and the FDA doesn't approve supplements for effectiveness before sale. "Shrinks the prostate" or "replaces your medication" are drug claims and a red flag.

The checklist

  1. Get symptoms evaluated first. A clinician can check for infection, diabetes, medication effects and prostate cancer.
  2. Read the Supplement Facts panel. Are amounts listed? Beware proprietary blends that hide doses.
  3. Look for human trials, not tradition. Search PubMed for the ingredient plus "randomized" and "lower urinary tract symptoms." Lab and animal studies don't show benefit in men.
  4. Match the dose to the studies. A trial at one extract dose says nothing about a product with an unspecified amount.
  5. Check safety and interactions with NIH ODS and NCCIH. Watch for iodine from seaweeds, blood-thinner interactions and heavy metals.
  6. Look for independent testing. USP Verified and NSF Certified marks mean a third party checked identity, amount and contaminants. "Quality standards" on the seller's own page is not the same.
  7. Scrutinize the marketing. Warning signs: unverifiable testimonials, unnamed "doctor" quotes, countdown timers, percentages with no data, "secret" ingredients, and an unclear seller. Some men's-health products have been found to contain undeclared drugs, so check FDA warnings.
  8. Understand the refund and who is behind it. A guarantee is only as good as the seller. Look for a physical address and clear return terms.

What guidelines recommend instead

The American Urological Association's BPH guideline centers on evaluation, lifestyle changes such as less evening fluid and caffeine, prescription medicines (alpha-blockers, 5-alpha-reductase inhibitors, tadalafil where appropriate) and procedures when needed.
